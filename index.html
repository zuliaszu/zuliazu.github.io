About

I am the EMEA security lead for the AWS Generative AI Innovation Center: the security authority on GenAI engagements across the region, and the escalation point when security decides whether an AI project goes ahead.

I have spent eight years in engineering, DevOps and security for regulated enterprises, the last five in security. I have run more than 45 engagement security assessments for banks, insurers, automotive finance, pharma, energy and public sector organisations across the UK, EU, Middle East and Africa.

My specialism is the part people find hardest: EU data residency, data retention and Zero Data Retention, GDPR and DPIA-aligned data classification, and the security of agentic systems, including MCP and autonomous agents. I spend my days with CISOs, DPOs, compliance officers and AI governance boards, translating what a frontier model can do into a deployment a regulator will accept.

Before security I built manufacturing scheduling software and taught algorithms and data structures at Durham University, where I read Computer Science.

Positions

How I think about security

Positions I keep coming back to, taken from things I have written and talked about.

  1. 01

    AI security overlaps, not replaces

    I see AI security and cybersecurity as overlapping fields, like a Venn diagram, not one subsuming the other. AI's probabilistic nature and growing autonomy still deserve tailored defences even where existing controls help.

  2. 02

    Data attacks start early

    The biggest risks to data are poisoning training data and manipulating features or datasets, and they show up at the earliest stages of an attack, not at the model. If training data is not validated, everything downstream inherits the problem.

  3. 03

    Supply chain is the whole pipeline

    A model file, a third-party library, a CI/CD pipeline, a cloud provider: any one of these can be the entry point, so I treat supply chain risk as running through the entire life cycle, not just the dependencies list.

  4. 04

    Structure follows headcount, not fashion

    There is no one-size-fits-all security org. Structure follows headcount and risk profile: work out which parts of the CIA triad your threat profile actually stresses, staff for that, and pick an org model on purpose rather than inheriting one.

  5. 05

    FDEs are an access question

    The forward deployed engineer model is an admission that the bottleneck moved from model capability to deployment reality. For anyone in security this matters twice: FDEs get deep access to production systems by design, and what they leave behind becomes part of the attack surface someone has to defend.

The reason is blunt: the hard part of AI is not the model.
